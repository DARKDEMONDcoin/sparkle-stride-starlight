# PLAYBOOK: LANDING PAGES (load for any page work and whenever conversion rate is weak)

## 14.27 Principles and priority order
- Message match between the ad/keyword and the page is the highest-leverage fix on most pages; when the promise breaks, visitors feel they are in the wrong place and leave. Build a separate page for any campaign whose message differs, because one page cannot match several promises at once. [E][V]
- Priority order: (1) message match, (2) speed and mobile, (3) form friction, (4) copy, call to action, and proof, (5) continuous testing. Score ideas by impact, confidence, and ease and do the top ones first. Do not start with colors or fonts. [V]
- Benchmark against the account's own pages by traffic source and device. Published average conversion rates differ widely by source and by what each study counts as a conversion, and mobile usually converts below desktop, so never use an external average as a target. [V]
- Every extra link is an exit; keep one primary goal per page and repeat that action. A page built for paid traffic should not have the navigation of a homepage. [V]
- Many practitioners also say to "run your own checkout": the page and checkout must work on a real phone. Ask the user to do this and report friction. [E]

## 14.28 Page audit checklist and thresholds
- Message match: headline repeats the ad's promise and key phrase; imagery and offer continue the ad. Dynamic text replacement can help at scale. [V]
- Above the fold: confirm relevance, state the main benefit, show proof, present the call to action, and load fast enough to be seen. [V]
- Forms: cutting fields usually raises completion; use a single column, easy fields first, make phone optional where possible, use multi-step for high-commitment offers, and qualify later; but match the form to the size of the decision, because length is not the only variable. [V][E]
- Proof: specific, result-anchored testimonials and numbers beat generic praise. [V]
- Copy: plain language, first-person call-to-action wording as a test idea, benefits over feature lists. [V]
- Speed and mobile: LCP at or under 2.5s, INP at or under 200ms, CLS at or under 0.1 at the 75th percentile of real users; prioritize mobile; common fixes are image compression, lazy loading below the fold, removing unnecessary third-party scripts, and a CDN. [G][V]
- Tracking and policy: presence of tags, no duplicate tags, consent behavior, HTTPS, redirects, broken links, language and direction, the offer in the ad present on the page, privacy policy. [E]
- Google quality diagnostics: keywords with poor landing-page experience, high-spend pages with poor conversion rates, and pages that don't match the ad promise or search intent point to a slow, unclear, generic, or mismatched page. Quality score is a diagnostic, not a goal; a low score does not always mean pause the keyword. [E]

## 14.29 Page-level diagnostics from ad data
- Meta: outbound clicks much higher than landing page views points to speed, a broken click path, or tracking; compare with the account's own ratio and trend. Good landing page views but weak view content or add-to-cart points to message match. Weak add-to-cart to purchase points to price, trust, shipping, or checkout. [V]
- Google: compare conversion rate and cost per conversion by final URL and expanded final URL, by device; find high-spend pages with weak conversion; find ad groups whose ads and keywords do not match the page. [E]
- Weak conversion with healthy tracking and a healthy click-to-view ratio is a message match, offer, or form problem; fix the page before raising bids or budgets. [E]

## 14.30 Testing pages
- Decide sample size or duration and success criteria before launch; no peeking; run whole weeks. At low traffic test bold changes (offer, page structure, value proposition) on the highest-traffic pages; button-color questions will not resolve at small scale. [V]
- Change one major element family at a time (message match, then form, then proof) so the effect can be attributed, and judge on lag-adjusted conversions by traffic source.
